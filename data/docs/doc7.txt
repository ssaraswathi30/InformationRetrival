Natural Language Processing and Computational Linguistics

Natural language processing enables computers to understand and generate human language. Computational linguistics applies linguistic theory to language technology development. NLP applications include machine translation, sentiment analysis, and question answering systems.

Text preprocessing in natural language processing involves tokenization, stemming, and lemmatization. Part-of-speech tagging identifies grammatical categories of words. Named entity recognition extracts person names, locations, and organizations from text.

Modern natural language processing relies on neural networks and transformer architectures. BERT and GPT models achieve state-of-the-art performance on language understanding tasks. These models learn contextual word representations from large text corpora.

Language models capture statistical patterns in text data. N-gram models estimate word sequence probabilities. Neural language models use recurrent or transformer architectures to generate coherent text.

Natural language processing challenges include handling ambiguity, understanding context, and processing multiple languages. Low-resource languages lack sufficient training data. Multilingual models enable cross-lingual transfer learning. Applications of natural language processing continue expanding with advances in deep learning and computational resources.
